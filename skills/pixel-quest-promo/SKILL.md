# Pixel Quest Promo

A 20-second, 1920×1080 promo that plays like an old platform game, in five scenes of two bars each at 120 BPM:

1. **Title screen (0–4 s).** The product name as a big pixel logo, a subtitle and a blinking prompt (`PRESS START`, or your own). On beat 7 the prompt is "pressed" and blinks fast; a pixel wipe opens the game.
2. **Stages 1–3 (4–16 s).** Each stage is one feature: `STAGE 1` and the feature name drop in, a dialog box types one line about it, and the hero runs in, collects three coins on the beat (+100 each) and jumps into a `?` block that pops out a power-up (+1000). Stage 1 is open hills, stage 2 mountains, stage 3 a castle at night (or at sunset, on a light sky).
3. **Ending (16–20 s).** Your headline in the big pixel font, a subline, the blinking prompt and your link, with fireworks and the hero jumping on the beat.

The HUD stays up throughout: score, coins, world and a countdown timer, all counting with the action.

Everything is drawn in code from text grids: a 5×7 pixel font, three heroes (robot, cat, knight) with walk and jump frames, eight power-ups, coins, blocks, hills, mountains and a castle, turned into crisp SVG. No images, no font files, nothing generated by AI. The template is a [HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) composition: you fill in a brief, a script writes it into the template, another checks the pixel text, and HyperFrames renders the MP4 on your machine.

There is no audio in the film. Every hit lands on the beat grid instead, and `cues.mjs` writes a cue sheet so music can be added in step 8.

## What you need

- Node.js 22 or newer and FFmpeg on the PATH.
- HyperFrames runs through `npx`, pinned to the version this template was tested with: `npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.192`.
- The first render downloads a headless Chrome. If `render` says Chrome is missing, run `npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.192 browser ensure` once.
- Network access to `cdn.jsdelivr.net`, which serves GSAP to the template. See Troubleshooting if it is blocked.

This skill's folder is `<skill>` below: the folder this SKILL.md is in.

## Steps

### 1. Get the copy

Pick the product's three best features: the ones a player would want as power-ups. Ask for anything you can't infer. Text is drawn in capitals in the pixel font, which has A–Z, 0–9, space and `. , ! ? - : / ' " & + % # @ ( ) $ * > < = × ·` (curly quotes and dashes are converted). No accents, emoji or other scripts.

| Field                        | What it is                                                  | Limit |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| `product`                    | The logo on the title screen                                | 12    |
| `subtitle`                   | Line under the logo; may be `""`                            | 24    |
| `cta`                        | The blinking prompt on the title and the ending             | 16    |
| `hero`                       | `robot`, `cat` or `knight`                                  | –     |
| `level1Name` … `level3Name`  | Each feature's name, the stage title                        | 14    |
| `level1Text` … `level3Text`  | One line about it, typed into the dialog box                | 30    |
| `level1Item` … `level3Item`  | The power-up: `bolt`, `star`, `heart`, `key`, `gem`, `shield`, `clock`, `chat` | – |
| `headline`                   | The big line on the ending                                  | 12    |
| `subline`                    | Line under it; may be `""`                                  | 20    |
| `url`                        | The link on the ending; may be `""`                         | 28    |
| `sky`, `ink`, `accent`, `grass` | Sky, text, logo and headline, ground (hex)               | –     |

Writing tips:

- Feature names are titles, not sentences: `FAST SEARCH`, `STREAK SHIELD`. The line under each says what it does for the user, with a number when there is a true one: `FIND ANY NOTE IN 0.1 S`.
- Match each power-up to its feature: `bolt` for speed, `shield` for privacy or safety, `key` for access, `clock` for time, `chat` for messaging or sharing, `heart` for health or favourites, `gem` for premium, `star` for anything else. Use three different ones.
- Headline and subline read as one line: `LEVEL UP` / `YOUR NOTES`.
- A stage holds for four seconds. Keep every line short enough to read in two.

### 2. Pick colours

Four hex colours. A dark `sky` gives night scenes with stars and a moon; a light `sky` gives daylight, a dusky pink stage and a sunset castle. `ink` is all the small text and must read on every scene's sky (shifted darker or lighter per stage); `accent` is the logo, the headline and the `STAGE` labels; `grass` is the ground.

| Preset     | sky       | ink       | accent    | grass     |
| ---------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- |
| Night Quest | `#1b1446` | `#f4f1ff` | `#3fb950` | `#2c8c3c` |
| Daylight   | `#9ad8f5` | `#1a1b3a` | `#c2185b` | `#4caf50` |
| Ember      | `#2a0f12` | `#fff1e0` | `#ffb02e` | `#a8452a` |
| Deep Sea   | `#0b2a33` | `#eafffb` | `#ff4fa3` | `#1f9e89` |
| Peach      | `#ffe9c7` | `#2b1d3a` | `#7a3cff` | `#6aa84f` |

Step 4 measures every pair and says which colour to change.

### 3. Make the project and apply the brief

```bash
cp -r <skill>/template ./pixel-quest
cd pixel-quest
```

Write `brief.json` in the project with every field from steps 1 and 2:

```json
{
  "product": "INKWELL",
  "subtitle": "A NOTES APP QUEST",
  "cta": "PRESS START",
  "hero": "robot",
  "level1Name": "QUICK CAPTURE",
  "level1Text": "NOTES SAVE AS YOU TYPE",
  "level1Item": "bolt",
  "level2Name": "FAST SEARCH",
  "level2Text": "FIND ANY NOTE IN 0.1 S",
  "level2Item": "gem",
  "level3Name": "PRIVATE VAULT",
  "level3Text": "END-TO-END ENCRYPTED",
  "level3Item": "shield",
  "headline": "LEVEL UP",
  "subline": "YOUR NOTES",
  "url": "INKWELL.EXAMPLE",
  "sky": "#1b1446",
  "ink": "#f4f1ff",
  "accent": "#3fb950",
  "grass": "#2c8c3c"
}
```

Then write it into the template:

```bash
node <skill>/scripts/apply-brief.mjs . brief.json
```

It checks every value against the template's declared variables (type, length, colour format, the hero and power-up names) and lists every problem at once if anything is off. Fix the brief and run it again. Fields you leave out keep their current value, so set all of them.

Two finished briefs are in `<skill>/examples/`, one night and one daylight.

### 4. Preflight the pixel text

```bash
node <skill>/scripts/preflight.mjs .
```

The film draws its own pixel font, so HyperFrames' text checks can't read it; this does those checks from the same font and palette the film uses. It lists every problem at once and exits 1 if there are any:

- characters the font can't draw, per field, so you can respell them (`É` → `E`, `→` → `>`);
- lines too wide for their place at the size they'll be drawn (the limits in step 1 make this rare);
- colour pairs that don't read: `ink` on each scene's sky and in the dialog box (4.5:1), `accent` on the title, stage and ending skies (3:1), and the ground against the sky.

`preflight: ok` means the copy and colours are safe. It may add notes, such as two stages sharing a power-up.

### 5. Check

```bash
npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.192 check --json
```

`"ok": true` means the composition lints, plays and lays out cleanly. One warning is expected and needs no fix: `film_silent`, because the film ships without sound (step 8). The contrast audit reports `0` text checks because there is no HTML text; step 4 covered that.

### 6. Look before you render

```bash
npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.192 snapshot --at 2.1,5.6,7.6,9.6,13.9,17.3 -o snapshots
```

Open the PNGs in `snapshots/`: the title screen, a stage mid-run, the power-up out of its block, the other two stages and the ending. Check that the logo and headline are big and centred, that the feature lines read in their dialog boxes, and that the power-ups suit their features.

### 7. Render

```bash
npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.192 render --quality delivery -o renders/pixel-quest-promo.mp4
```

`npm run check` and `npm run render` in the project do the same. Use `--quality draft` for a fast test.

### 8. Music (optional)

Write the cue sheet:

```bash
node <skill>/scripts/cues.mjs . -o cues.csv
```

It lists every beat of the 120 BPM, 4/4 grid (10 bars, first downbeat at 0.000 s) and every on-screen hit on the beat it lands on: scene cuts and wipes, stage banners, the three coin pickups, jumps, the block hits, fireworks. A musician can score to it, or you can pick any 120 BPM chiptune track that starts on a downbeat. To lay a track under the film:

```bash
ffmpeg -i renders/pixel-quest-promo.mp4 -i music.mp3 -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v copy -c:a aac -af "afade=t=out:st=19:d=1" -t 20 renders/pixel-quest-promo-music.mp4
```

Use music the person has the rights to. If the track has a pickup before its first downbeat, trim it first (`-ss` on the music input) so its bar 1 starts at 0.

### 9. Hand it off

Give the person `renders/pixel-quest-promo.mp4` (and the music version if you made one) and the title-screen still from step 6. For another product they only edit `brief.json` and rerun steps 3 to 7.

## Timing map

| Time (s)    | Beat (bar.beat) | What happens                                                  |
| ----------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0.00        | 1.1             | Title screen already up: logo, subtitle, hero, prompt         |
| 0.00–3.00   | 1.1–2.3         | Prompt blinks on every beat; hero bobs                        |
| 3.00–3.75   | 2.3–2.4         | Prompt "pressed": fast blink                                  |
| 3.75–4.25   | 2.4–3.1         | Pixel wipe into stage 1                                       |
| +0.13–0.38  | x.1             | Stage label and name drop in                                  |
| +0.50–1.50  | x.2–x.4         | Dialog types the feature line                                 |
| +1.00, 1.50, 2.00 | x.3, x.4, y.1 | Coins collected, +100 each                                |
| +2.50–3.00  | y.2             | Jump; hits the block on the "and" (+2.75): power-up, +1000    |
| +3.00       | y.3             | Scrolling stops; hero hops on y.4                             |
| 8.00, 12.00 | 5.1, 7.1        | Stages 2 and 3 start (same beats, new scenery)                |
| 16.00       | 9.1             | Ending: headline, then subline, prompt and link               |
| 16.5, 17.5, 18.5 | 9.2, 9.4, 10.2 | Fireworks                                               |
| 17, 18, 19  | 9.3, 10.1, 10.3 | Hero jumps                                                    |

`+` times are from each stage's first downbeat (4, 8 and 12 s); x and y are its two bars. `cues.csv` from step 8 has the exact list.

## Several versions at once

To render one promo per product or language, write a JSON array of briefs and render them in one go. Each object is merged over the values already applied:

```bash
npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.192 render --batch variants.json --quality delivery -o "renders/{product}.mp4"
```

`--batch` skips `apply-brief` and `preflight`, so run both on each brief first (apply it, preflight, move on to the next), then batch-render.

## Troubleshooting

- **`preflight` says a character can't be drawn.** Respell it with the characters listed in step 1. Text is always capitals, so case doesn't matter.
- **The logo or headline looks small.** They shrink to fit the frame: up to 7 characters draw at full size, 12 at a little over half. Shorter is bigger.
- **Render hangs, then shows a static frame, or reports `sub_timeline_script_failure`.** GSAP didn't load. If your network blocks `cdn.jsdelivr.net`, run `npm i gsap@3.14.2` in the project, copy `node_modules/gsap/dist/gsap.min.js` to `assets/vendor/`, and point the `<script src>` in `index.html` at `assets/vendor/gsap.min.js`.
- **Colours didn't change.** Run `apply-brief` again and check it printed `Applied N values`. Editing the CSS `:root` colours has no effect; the variables win.
- **The music isn't 120 BPM.** The film is timed to 120 BPM and 20 seconds throughout. Pick a 120 BPM track, or time-stretch yours (`-af atempo=` in FFmpeg) rather than changing the template's beat map.

## Files

- `template/`: the HyperFrames project. `index.html` holds the variables and four layers; `quest-art.js` holds the font, sprites, palette and beat map as plain data; `quest.js` draws every frame from the time alone.
- `scripts/apply-brief.mjs`: validates a brief and writes it into the template. No dependencies.
- `scripts/preflight.mjs`: checks the pixel text's characters, widths and colours. No dependencies.
- `scripts/cues.mjs`: writes the beat grid and on-screen hits as a CSV cue sheet. No dependencies.
- `scripts/load-project.mjs`: reads a project's applied values and art module for the two scripts above.
- `examples/inkwell-launch/` and `examples/hopscotch/`: finished briefs, night and daylight. The previews on Lookbk are rendered from these.
- `NOTICE.md`: third-party software this skill uses.
